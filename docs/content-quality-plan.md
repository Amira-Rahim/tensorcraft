## The outcome a buyer should receive

A JavaScript engineer with no ML background should finish able to frame a
small ML problem, prepare data without leakage, compare a model against a
simple baseline, evaluate failures, and run their saved model in a working
JavaScript application. They should understand when the result is unreliable
and when an ordinary rule is sufficient.

The deliverable is a complete, readable course with useful feedback,
a satisfying story, and a project the learner can run, explain, and modify
outside Tensorcraft. It includes the guided exercises, an early transfer
assignment, and a final independent project. Completion does not imply
professional ML mastery, an accredited qualification, or a salary gain.

## What 9/10 means

These are proposed product acceptance criteria, not published industry
benchmarks. Freeze them before evaluating the revised course.

Each row has five criteria, scored 0, 1, or 2: missing/incorrect, useful but
inconsistent, or consistently demonstrated. A category passes at 9/10 or
better, with no zero. Do not average categories to hide a weak one. Each
applicable independent reviewer must reach the threshold; unresolved
disagreements need evidence and another review. Unreviewed categories stay
unrated. The author cannot supply the final independent score.

| Category | Five criteria, two points each | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit for JavaScript engineers | Honest prerequisites; concepts explained before use; runnable JS examples; accurate bridges with limits; clear progression from JS to ML | Full curriculum map and observed readers from browser and Node backgrounds |
| Learning design | Objectives match practice; scaffolding decreases; feedback explains errors; retrieval checks understanding; later tasks require earlier skills | Exercise review, unseen transfer work, and delayed recall |
| Factual reliability | Correct claims; correct assumptions and units; body/recap/exercise agreement; reproducible demonstrations; qualified limitations | Every lesson and exercise reviewed, a claim ledger, and no unresolved consequential errors |
| Readability and voice | Clear main points; introduced jargon; manageable sentence detail; purposeful repetition; distinct dialogue | Full editorial pass and fresh readers explaining passages in their own words |
| Storytelling | Coherent mystery; developing characters; technical work causes events; stakes have consequences; the ending pays off the course | Season continuity review and learner accounts of motives, changes, and payoff |
| Player agency | Choices use evidence; options have costs; consequences persist; later scenes acknowledge them; outcomes stay scientifically honest | Saved-choice browser tests and learners identifying later effects of decisions |
| Independent application | Frame a new problem; choose a valid split; compare baseline/model; diagnose failures; export and run the result | An unfamiliar project completed without a supplied solution, assessed with a public rubric |
| Value at the current price | Useful scope; dependable practice; usable project artifact; clear access/cost/support terms; real willingness to pay | Quality gates below, interviews, and actual $79 introductory and $99 regular purchase/refund evidence |
| Learner ROI | Demonstrated skill gain; retention; transfer; manageable time investment; honest outcome claims | Pre/post/delayed tasks, observed effort, project review, and follow-up use |
| Readiness to pursue 150,000 users | Defined target; measurable funnel; repeatable acquisition; sustainable delivery economics; demand for another world | Cohort and channel evidence. This scores readiness, not the probability of reaching 150,000 |

A wrong foundational rule, misleading guarantee, grader that rewards a
materially wrong solution, or advertised capability the course cannot
demonstrate blocks the relevant category regardless of its total score.
Fix confirmed teaching errors immediately, without waiting for a user count.

## Q2. Make the teaching produce transferable skills

Map each module from prerequisite to objective, explanation, practice,
feedback, and later use. Check foundations explicitly: training versus
inference; labels and features; baseline; loss versus evaluation metric;
training/validation/test; leakage; generalization; uncertainty; model/data
limitations. Repair missing foundations before adding advanced topics.

Keep the broad curriculum, but mark what learners must be able to do and
what is an optional deeper explanation. A first course can introduce
attention or Kalman filtering without implying mastery. Keep essential
assumptions in the main path when derivations move into drawers.

Progress from a worked example to a variation, then failure diagnosis, then
an unfamiliar task. Apply this at module scale, not as another mandatory
lesson template. Review all 65 exercises for taught prerequisites, honest
difficulty, useful feedback, and valid alternative solutions. Reduce hints
gradually; preserve explicit input/output contracts. Grade the objective
rather than a preferred spelling or API unless that API is the objective.

Add an early transfer assignment after the first trained classifier. Give
unfamiliar data and an outcome. Require a baseline, split rationale, metric,
and error analysis without prescribing the implementation. Use different
data and wording from the teaching example. Provide an optional rescue path
and record assistance so completion remains distinct from independent success.

Check rendered glossary coverage, including terms inside dialogue and
drawers. A term should be understandable at first use without opening
several pages. Reuse existing recall and review scheduling; improve prompts
that only reward remembering a story number or phrase.

Acceptance: each objective has practice and assessment; each major
misconception has a diagnostic task; a fresh reader can begin each exercise
without an untaught API. Independent transfer is measured in Q6.

## Q3. Edit the full course for clear, human-readable prose

Edit in this order: correctness, teaching clarity, then voice. Calibrate on
foundations, one dense middle module, and the capstone before applying the
style to all 56 lessons and 65 exercises.

Review all 206 recap bullets, starting with the 43 containing at least 50
words. Aim for one retrievable idea per bullet, usually 25-30 words or less.
Review exceptions above 40 words individually. These are review signals,
not permission to delete a necessary qualification. Keep detailed arithmetic
and benchmark methods available next to the claim they support.

Remove defensive explanations about defeating the grader from the reading
path. Keep the contract, examples, and actionable feedback. Resolve channel,
feature, model-size, latency, and budget terminology at the point of use so
the learner does not have to reconcile prior editorial repairs.

Read explanations aloud. Split overloaded sentences; replace vague claims
with an operation or observation. Retain necessary ML vocabulary and explain
it. Inspect unsupported absolutes and exact figures. Vary scenes where the
same analogy/correction/cost exchange is doing no new work. Do not use an
AI-authorship detector or a generated percentage as a quality score.

Acceptance: full editorial coverage; no unresolved high-impact clarity
finding; technical reviewer rechecks changed meaning; fresh readers explain
sampled passages without author assistance. Lexicon and format tests support
this review but do not replace it.

## Q5. Deliver a project worth taking out of the course

Keep the guided capstone as preparation. Add an independent project with a
supplied, licensed dataset unfamiliar to the learner, plus an option to
propose suitable data. Use a modest problem that runs on an ordinary laptop.
Establish provenance and group/time fields before selecting the split; a
fictional sensor label is not real-world data.

Require a justified baseline and metric, training-only preprocessing,
reproducible evaluation, failure examples, limitations, saved weights and
preprocessing metadata, and a working JS application loading the artifact
for inference. Require one adaptation to new data or a changed operating
constraint so copying a tutorial is insufficient.

Provide a learner-owned export with dependencies, lockfile, startup commands,
data instructions/license, README, evaluation report, and model card. A clean
download or clone must build and predict without Tensorcraft session storage,
hidden fixtures, or maintainer credentials. Check browser preprocessing
against training. Offer deployment instructions; local execution is required
so hosting purchases remain optional.

Publish a rubric covering problem/data, evaluation, modeling judgment,
implementation, and explanation, two points each. Pass at 8/10 with no
leakage, invalid evaluation, or missing external inference. Keep assessment
solutions hidden until submission or an explicit reveal. Distinguish
automated checks from expert judgment about problem framing. If assisted
coding is used, require an explanation and a fresh change; report assistance
rather than implying unaided mastery.

Extend [GitHub export](../apps/api/src/lib/github.ts) for the integrated
project, with a download for learners who do not connect GitHub. Exercise
commits and in-memory model export do not satisfy this outcome. Do not make
paid LLM calls necessary to pass; optional services need visible costs and
a local/test alternative.

Acceptance: an independent reviewer runs a clean export, reproduces its
evaluation, and sees a learner explain a failure and make a small change.
Advertise this outcome only after the path works.

## Q6. Validate with independent reviewers and real learners

Implementation update, 2026-09-18: the [study packet](learning-study/README.md)
provides reviewer instructions, scoring anchors, participant forms and an
offline record/summary tool. It prepares this phase; recruitment, independent
scores and learner observations remain open. The project GitHub exporter is
deployed. Its live publication, identical retry, clean-clone evaluation and
standalone browser predictions passed using a labeled maintainer fixture.
See the [live export results](audits/project-export-live-2026-09-18.md).
This verifies the export infrastructure, not independent learner achievement.

Obtain subject and editorial reviews by people who did not write the revised
material. Review every module, including exercises and answers. Give reviewers
the frozen rubric and evidence without asking them to confirm an author's
score. Fix disagreements and re-review affected areas. Model-assisted review
can find issues; it does not stand in for learners or external experts.

Run a diagnostic pilot with 12-20 JavaScript engineers new to ML. Include
browser and Node backgrounds and people outside the owner's personal circle.
Observe the beginning, early transfer task, a dense later module, and final
project. Follow the cohort through the course at a declared, realistic weekly
workload. Allow them to leave and record why.

Use a short pre-task, different post-task, and delayed check seven days later.
Assess training/inference, leakage, metric choice, confidence, and adapting a
working system. Record hints, solution reveals, outside help, active time,
confusion, and abandonment. Interview for concrete examples, not only liking
the course. Obtain consent and avoid recording unrelated personal/work data.
At a 30-day follow-up, ask whether learners used or adapted the project and
what they could do without the course open. Report non-response and observed
use separately from intentions. This supports the practical ROI assessment.

Proposed diagnostic gates to freeze before enrollment:

- At least 80% of first-module starters complete it without live author
  intervention. Normal in-product hints are allowed and reported.
- At least 80% of early transfer submitters score 8/10 or better without a
  solution reveal or critical leakage/evaluation error. Also report the share
  of all enrolled learners who reach and pass the task.
- At least 70% of the full-course cohort finish a runnable final project at
  8/10 or better within the declared study window. Investigate non-completion;
  do not silently drop it from the denominator.
- At least 75% of completers return the delayed check, and 80% of respondents
  answer at least four of five concept/application tasks correctly.
- At least 80% of observed readers explain sampled core passages without
  clarification and identify a meaningful story consequence. Review every
  repeated confusion pattern, including those limited to one JS background.

Publish counts alongside percentages. With 12 learners, one changes a rate
by over eight percentage points. These gates locate problems, not establish
population-wide efficacy. Revise failures and test with fresh participants
using different tasks. Do not lower thresholds after seeing results or
substitute satisfaction for demonstrated skill gain.
