# Independent reviewer criteria

This public review copy retains the selection criteria and scope from the
September 17, 2026 preparation record. It omits candidate names, personal
sites, email addresses, and contact routes. The owner still needs to choose
recipients, confirm independence and availability, and set a budget and
deadline before any outreach or engagement.

## Subject reviewer

Look for demonstrated work with TensorFlow.js, JavaScript-based training,
and browser or Node deployment. Relevant evidence includes executed technical
examples, published teaching material, and reviews that explain numerical or
evaluation defects. General ML experience alone does not establish familiarity
with the course's runtime and exercise contracts.

The reviewer should inspect preprocessing, split membership, leakage,
evaluation metrics, exercise grading, and exported inference. Ask for a
bounded paid sample and a separate full-course quote. Confirm relevant prior
work and disclose any competing course, consulting, or commercial interest.

## Editorial and instructional reviewer

Look for technical editing and programming-course experience, plus evidence
of clear instructional prose. Story or fiction editing experience can help
with character continuity and narrative pacing. Confirm comfort reading
JavaScript examples and reviewing a rendered interactive lesson.

The editorial reviewer should distinguish sentence-level edits from teaching
sequence problems. Ask for sample feedback that preserves technical meaning,
explains recurring readability issues, and checks whether scenes support the
learner's task. A separate subject reviewer should verify executable ML
claims unless one person can demonstrate both sets of skills.

## Proposed first engagement

Request a paid, fixed-scope sample before assigning the full course. The owner
sets the spending cap after receiving a quote. No amount is authorized by this
document. Give reviewers the same frozen sample and the relevant rubric,
without the previous author's numeric ratings.

- Subject sample: `foundations/05-normalization.mdx`,
  `anomaly-detection/01-normal-vs-anomalous.mdx`, the anomaly reconstruction
  exercise, and room-project brief/rubric. Require one executed example,
  evaluation/leakage inspection, and an evidence-linked finding list.
- Editorial sample: the same two lessons, including their dialogue and
  learner-facing instructions. Require annotated edits, explanations of
  recurring readability issues, and a short check of story/technical
  integration.

A useful sample finds and explains issues, distinguishes serious defects from
preferences, preserves accurate technical meaning, and records uncertainty.
The recorded full-course scope covers 56 lessons, 65 exercises, two external
projects, and their supporting interfaces. Refresh the handoff against the
current source before commissioning that scope.

The September 17 estimate counted about 77,800 word-like tokens in the 56 MDX
files after removing frontmatter, fenced code, and JSX tags. That rough count
includes some inline syntax and excludes other course surfaces. It is a
historical planning estimate, not a current billable word count. Ask the editor
to quote from frozen files and state which code, UI, dialogue, exercises, and
correction rounds the quote includes.

## Questions before appointment

Ask who will do the work, what relevant review samples they can share, which
conflicts they have, and which tools or AI assistance they plan to use. Agree
the earliest start, turnaround, fee/cap, included correction rounds, and
delivery format. Require human verification of executable claims and clear
disclosure of model-assisted work.

Agree how source files will be shared and removed afterward. Review access
must exclude customer data, production credentials, and repository write
permission. The [reviewer guide](reviewer-guide.md) and
[study overview](README.md) provide the existing review process.

[Reusable inquiry templates](reviewer-outreach-drafts.md) contain no chosen
recipients or contact details. Owner approval of recipients, wording, and
budget remains necessary before sending or commissioning work.
