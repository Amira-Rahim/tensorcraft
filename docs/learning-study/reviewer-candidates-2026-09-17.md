## Proposed first engagement

Request a paid, fixed-scope sample before assigning the full course. The owner
sets the spending cap after receiving a quote; no amount is authorized yet.
Give each candidate the same frozen sample and the relevant rubric, without
the previous author's numeric ratings.

- Subject sample: `foundations/05-normalization.mdx`,
  `anomaly-detection/01-normal-vs-anomalous.mdx`, the anomaly reconstruction
  exercise and room-project brief/rubric. Require one executed example,
  evaluation/leakage inspection and an evidence-linked finding list.
- Editorial sample: the same two lessons, including their dialogue and
  learner-facing instructions. Require annotated edits, explanations of
  recurring readability issues and a short check of story/technical integration.

A useful sample finds and explains issues, distinguishes serious defects from
preferences, preserves accurate technical meaning and records uncertainty.
The full engagement covers 56 lessons, 65 exercises, the two external projects
and their supporting interfaces. The current private review sheet has 212
artifact records; many are supporting files rather than lessons.

For estimating only, the 56 MDX files contain about 77,800 word-like tokens
after removing frontmatter, fenced code and JSX tags. This rough count still
includes some inline syntax and excludes other course surfaces. It is not a
billable word count. Ask the editor to quote from the frozen files and state
what code, UI, dialogue, exercises and re-review are included.

## Questions to settle before appointment

Ask for the named person doing the work, relevant review samples, conflicts,
tools/AI assistance they plan to use, earliest start, turnaround, fee/cap,
included correction rounds and delivery format. Require human verification
of executable claims and clear disclosure of model-assisted work. Agree how
source files are shared and removed afterward. Review access must not include
customer data, production credentials or repository write permission.
